A personal reading journal on Alzheimer’s disease and aging — two research areas I follow closely. Here I share recent papers and brief reflections on what they add to the field.

September 28–October 4, 2026

This week in context

  • AD measurement and regulation: Speech age gaps and functional-connectivity patterns distinguish complementary aspects of dementia and brain aging. [1] [2]
  • Molecular networks: Single-cell RNA processing and tauopathy protein-complex analyses extend the view beyond conventional gene expression. [3] [4]
  • Cellular aging mechanisms: Chromatin organization, intervention-responsive transcriptomic modules, and lysosomal lipid handling connect molecular changes with aging phenotypes. [5] [6] [7]
  • Early clinical evidence: Small studies of senolytics in MASH and fenofibrate after brain injury offer condition-specific signals, not established general anti-aging treatments. [8] [9]
  • Experimental intervention pathways: Worm stress responses, exercise-associated vesicles, muscle cardiolipin, and marrow-adipocyte senescence highlight distinct tissue and model contexts. [10] [11] [12] [13]

Alzheimer’s Disease  ·  Aging

Alzheimer’s Disease

01 · Science Advances · 2026-09-30
Human cross-sectional speech biomarker study

Speech clocks decode dementia phenotypes, social exposome, and biological aging.

Hernandez H, Pedraza LK, Santamaria-Garcia H et al.

Speech models trained on 2,928 people across five Latin American countries produced age-gap measures that differed across dementia groups. Associations with cognition, p-tau217, brain measures, and epigenetic clocks support further biomarker research, but the cross-sectional design does not establish prediction of future decline.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42814823

02 · Nature Neuroscience · 2026-10-01
Human functional-connectivity cohorts

Different functional connectivity gradients reflect aging and Alzheimer's disease.

Rittmo J, Franzmeier N, Strandberg O et al.

In BioFINDER-2 and ADNI, involving 973 and 129 participants, respectively, AD-related connectivity changes aligned with the sensory–association axis. Age-related changes followed a different representation–executive axis, suggesting distinct large-scale patterns rather than a single shared signature of aging and AD.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42823476

03 · Nature Genetics · 2026-09-30
Human single-cell regulatory genomics

Single-cell profiling and genetic regulation of alternative polyadenylation in the human brain.

Nan J, Boix CA, Shi S et al.

An atlas of two million cells from 379 brains with and without AD mapped alternative polyadenylation and its genetic regulation. AD-associated changes implicated pathways including microglial activation and neuronal transport, providing a regulatory layer complementary to conventional gene-expression analysis.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42816601

04 · Cell Reports · 2026-10-01
Experimental tauopathy interactomics

Progressive remodeling of global protein interaction networks in a mouse model of tauopathy.

Lin W, Phanse S, van der Spek SJF et al.

Time-resolved proteomics and phosphoproteomics mapped protein-complex remodeling in a mouse tauopathy model. Functional analyses implicated selected assemblies in early disease phenotypes, offering a resource for studying phosphorylation-dependent network changes without establishing efficacy in human AD.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42826098

Aging

05 · Science Advances · 2026-10-02
Mouse naive CD4+ T cell aging

Aging-associated SATB1 deficiency remodels 3D genome architecture and transcriptional programs in naive CD4+ T cells.

Wang B, Ji L, Bian Q et al.

Aged naive CD4+ T cells showed weakened chromatin-domain boundaries, rewired enhancer–promoter interactions, and increased inflammatory gene expression. Reduced SATB1 contributed to these changes; conditional deletion reproduced aspects of the aging-associated genome architecture and transcriptional profile.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42826184

06 · Science Advances · 2026-09-30
Transcriptomic screening + worm lifespan experiments

Predicting and finding geroprotective compounds through modulating conserved longevity-associated aging modules.

Huai W, Ng LF, Lee JH et al.

Coexpression modules responsive to lifespan-extending interventions guided compound selection. Ten predicted compounds extended lifespan in C. elegans, including seven previously unreported candidates; several modules were conserved in mice and correlated with frailty. Human efficacy was not tested.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42814817

07 · Cell Reports · 2026-10-01
Mouse brain lysosomal multiomics

Lysosomal multi-omics reveals altered sphingolipid catabolism associated with lysosomal dysfunction in the aging brain.

Sarkar C, Chen Y, Nguyen DP et al.

Lysosomal lipidomics and proteomics across mice aged 3–24 months identified accumulation of myelin components and altered sphingolipid catabolism. Experimental analyses linked myelin-derived glycosphingolipid accumulation to lysosomal enlargement and dysfunction, most prominently in microglia.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42826099

08 · Nature Metabolism · 2026-10-01
Small randomized trial in fibrotic MASH

Senolytics dasatinib and quercetin in metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis: a proof-of-principle randomized, controlled trial.

Koning M, Kovynev A, Fondevila MF et al.

A placebo-controlled trial randomized 31 participants to intermittent dasatinib plus quercetin or placebo. Fibrosis improvement without MASH worsening occurred in 47% versus 7%, while adverse events were more frequent with treatment. The proof-of-principle results support larger trials, not routine anti-aging use.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42823536

09 · Science Translational Medicine · 2026-09-30
Age-related neuroinflammation + pilot clinical trial

Cranial bone marrow-derived monocytes promote neuroinflammation in chronic traumatic brain injury.

Lu Z, Wang C, Gong P et al.

Experiments linked age-related PPARα deficiency in cranial marrow-derived monocytes to inflammatory lipid-metabolic changes after chronic traumatic brain injury. A 40-person proof-of-concept trial in older adults reported reduced plasma neurofilament light and improved cognition with fenofibrate; broader clinical benefit requires confirmation.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42814802

10 · Cell Reports · 2026-09-30
Worm mitochondrial stress and longevity

USP-48 enables insulin-mediated longevity and modulates the mitochondrial stress response.

Gordillo-García S, Fernandez-Abascal J, Hernando-Rodríguez B et al.

A double-RNAi screen identified USP-48 as a regulator of mitochondrial stress responses and lifespan extension under reduced insulin signaling. Tissue-specific analyses and histone deubiquitination experiments connected stress-responsive transcription with context-dependent longevity in C. elegans.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42814573

11 · Nature Aging · 2026-09-29
Older-adult exercise intervention + cartilage models

Exercise enhances aged chondrocyte health through microRNA-29-enriched extracellular vesicles.

Iijima H, Dave KM, Wang K et al.

Three months of aerobic exercise increased extracellular-vesicle microRNA-29 relative to baseline in older adults. Cellular experiments implicated KL derepression, and exercise-primed vesicles improved cartilage health in aged mice. These findings do not establish the efficacy of vesicle injections in humans.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42811122

12 · Nature Aging · 2026-09-29
Human muscle observations + mouse mechanisms

Mitochondrial membrane lipid cardiolipin controls fiber-type adaptations in aging muscle via estrogen-related receptor γ.

Finger F, Frost M, Watanabe S et al.

Declining cardiolipin synthesis was linked to aging-related muscle fiber adaptations. Inducible Crls1 deletion in mice reproduced key changes, while restoring expression replenished cardiolipin, initiated reversal of atrophy, and rescued premature mortality in the knockout model.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42811121

13 · Nature Communications · 2026-09-29
Stress-induced adipocyte senescence in mice

Pulsed electromagnetic fields activate a peripheral interoceptive pathway to suppress sympathetic Npy for osteogenesis.

Wang T, Liang Z, Zeng W et al.

In male mice, sympathetic neuropeptide Y promoted marrow-adipocyte senescence and impaired bone formation under chronic stress. Pulsed electromagnetic fields reduced bone loss through a sensory–sympathetic circuit involving semaphorin 3A, supporting a local mechanism rather than established treatment for human aging.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42811065

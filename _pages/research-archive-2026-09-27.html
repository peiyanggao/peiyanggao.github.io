September 21–27, 2026

This week in context

  • AD mechanisms and measurement: TREM2 activation in mice, human navigation strategies, and organoid exosome profiling offer complementary experimental and measurement approaches. [1] [2] [3]
  • Pathological transitions and living tissue: Brain-network stratification and surgical biopsies connect AD staging with spatial pathology and protein kinetics, although treatment-stratification findings remain exploratory. [4] [5]
  • Cellular heterogeneity in AD: Population-scale single-nucleus analyses, genetic regulation atlases, differential-expression methods, and donor-specific networks emphasize distinct cellular and individual contexts. [6] [7] [8] [9]
  • Systemic aging: Oral microbiome experiments, kidney epigenomics, menopause proteomics, and the liver–cartilage selenium axis connect tissue-specific biology with broader aging processes. Observational associations and animal interventions require different interpretations. [10] [11] [12] [13]
  • Measuring senescent and aging states: RamanOmics and cell-composition modeling expand the tools for studying spatial senescence and age-associated cellular changes. [14] [15]

Alzheimer’s Disease

01 · Cell Reports · 2026-09-26
Microglial modulation in AD mice

Activation of TREM2 via single-domain antibodies improves synaptic and cognitive function in 5×FAD mice.

Yu X, Ren Q, Gao Y et al.

Two TREM2-activating single-domain antibodies improved cognition after hippocampal injection in early-stage 5×FAD mice. Both protected synapses and reduced C1q; only 10E12 reduced amyloid plaques, indicating that the observed synaptic benefits were not uniformly accompanied by plaque reduction.

02 · Cell Reports · 2026-09-25
Human navigation + neurophysiology

Navigation strategies predict spatial memory and hippocampal recruitment.

Patai EZ, Stawarczyk D, Herweg N et al.

Virtual-navigation experiments included epilepsy patients, young APOE-ε4 carriers, and healthy controls. Trajectory measures revealed variable, adaptive strategies and group-dependent hippocampal recruitment, offering a behavioral perspective on genetic AD vulnerability without establishing prediction of future dementia.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42789386

03 · Science Advances · 2026-09-23
iPSC-derived cortical organoids + exosome profiling

Ultrasound-enhanced exosome secretion and antibody-free SERS profiling for Alzheimer's disease via iPSC-derived cortical organoids.

Yoo J, Kim Y, Yu WJ et al.

Optimized ultrasound stimulation increased exosome secretion from cortical organoids, strengthening label-free surface-enhanced Raman scattering signals. Exosomal fingerprints distinguished APOE genotype-dependent differences; this organoid-based platform does not yet establish diagnostic performance in patients.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42777042

04 · Science Advances · 2026-09-23
Human neuroimaging + proteomics

Beyond statistical thresholds: Mapping sequential ATN cascade transitions via brain structural topography in Alzheimer's disease.

Wang D, Yan S, Zhou R et al.

A brain-network framework identified four reproducible subgroups with distinct biomarker profiles and longitudinal cognitive trajectories. Proteomic analyses suggested sequential biological transitions. A post hoc treatment analysis linked the initiation-stage subgroup to slower decline after amyloid-targeting therapy, warranting prospective validation.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42777020

05 · Nature Communications · 2026-09-24
Living human brain biopsies + protein kinetics

Spatiotemporal characterization of Alzheimer disease pathology in living human brain tissue.

Aslanyan A, Mukherjee S, Moncur EM et al.

Biopsies from 18 people undergoing shunt surgery for suspected normal pressure hydrocephalus enabled spatial and kinetic analysis of AD pathology. Nine contained amyloid plaques; p-tau217 was enriched nearby. Isotope labeling estimated brain tau half-life at about 34 days, whereas plaques showed little detectable turnover.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42786174

06 · Nature Medicine · 2026-09-23
Population-scale human single-nucleus transcriptomics

AI-based characterization of Alzheimer's disease phenotypes from population-scale single-cell data.

He C, Li AZ, Hanthanan Arachchilage K et al.

PASCode identified approximately 1.5 million phenotype-associated cells from 584 donors with AD-related phenotypes. Analyses prioritized microglial and astrocytic subpopulations and pathways associated with pathology, cognitive resilience, and impairment, with validation in external datasets. These are computationally prioritized associations, not proven therapeutic targets.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42778763

07 · Nature Genetics · 2026-09-23
Human cell-type-specific genetic regulation

Single-nucleus atlas of cell-type specific genetic regulation in the human brain.

Zeng B, Yang H, N M P et al.

An atlas of 5.6 million nuclei from 1,384 donors mapped genetic regulation across prefrontal cortical cell types. Colocalization identified cell-type-specific genes implicated in AD and other disorders that bulk analyses missed, providing a resource for linking genetic risk to cellular context.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42778721

08 · Nature Communications · 2026-09-23
Statistical methods + human AD brain data

Efficient differential expression analysis of large-scale single-cell transcriptomics data using Dreamlet.

Hoffman GE, Lee D, Bendl J et al.

Dreamlet uses precision-weighted pseudobulk linear mixed models for differential expression across donors and cell clusters. Evaluation included 1.4 million nuclei from 150 AD cases and 149 controls, demonstrating computational efficiency and false-positive control while accommodating complex study designs.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42778542

09 · Nature Communications · 2026-09-23
Personalized human functional genomics

Personalized single-cell transcriptomics reveals molecular diversity in Alzheimer's disease.

Chandrashekar PB, Alatkar SA, Cohen Kalafut N et al.

The iBrainMap framework analyzed donor-level regulatory networks and cellular interactions, with cross-cohort validation spanning more than 1,900 brains. Graph-based representations classified AD phenotypes and identified molecular subgroups, while regulatory QTL analyses linked genetic variation to donor-specific network changes.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42778537

Aging

10 · Nature Aging · 2026-09-24
Human multiomics + worm and mouse experiments

Multi-modality profiling identifies Neisseria flavescens as a central geroprotective oral commensal in humans.

Chen J, Ren Y, Zhou Y et al.

A multi-modality aging framework identified oral Neisseria flavescens as associated with decelerated human aging. Live bacteria extended lifespan and healthspan in worms; heat-killed supplementation shifted molecular profiles toward younger states in aged mice. Human intervention efficacy remains unestablished.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42786310

11 · Nature Aging · 2026-09-24
Human and mouse kidney single-cell multiomics

A cross-species single-cell kidney epigenome atlas reveals epithelial-dominant aging-like states in disease.

Jeong H, Lake BB, Diep D et al.

A cross-species atlas linked diseased tubular epithelial states to accelerated epigenetic aging and transcriptional trajectories seen in aged kidneys. DNA methylation, chromatin accessibility, and three-dimensional genome analyses localized these changes to unresolved repair states and disrupted epithelial identity.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42786309

12 · Nature Medicine · 2026-09-22
Human menopause proteomics + longitudinal cohorts

Blood proteomics of menopause map to brain aging and dementia risk.

Wood Alexander M, Rabin JS, Caunca M et al.

Proteomic analyses of 80 women, followed by validation in 2,814 women, linked menopause to inflammatory, synaptic, metabolic, and aging-related signatures. Across four older-women cohorts totaling 11,925 participants, higher menopause proteomic scores were associated with cognitive aging and dementia risk.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42773212

13 · Cell Metabolism · 2026-09-22
Human cohorts + experimental cartilage senescence

A liver-cartilage selenium axis underlies metabolic comorbidity in osteoarthritis.

Yong S, Jo H, Yook G et al.

Human cohorts linked liver disease with osteoarthritis prevalence. Mouse and mechanistic experiments implicated reduced hepatic SELENOP output in impaired selenium delivery to cartilage and chondrocyte senescence. Selenium supplementation restored cartilage selenoproteins and alleviated disease in models, not yet demonstrating prevention in humans.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42772285

14 · Nature Aging · 2026-09-21
Mouse tissue aging and repair + multimodal profiling

RamanOmics decodes the spatial vibrational-molecular architecture of senescence in aging and repair.

Zhang K, Chen X, Monticolo F et al.

RamanOmics combined Raman imaging with single-nucleus and spatial transcriptomics in young and old mouse lung and skin. It identified tissue-specific senescence programs and a conserved lipid-linked signature in p21-positive cells, enabling nondestructive spatial profiling and analysis of senescent states during wound healing.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42768111

15 · Nature Communications · 2026-09-23
Statistical methods applied to aging datasets

Fast, flexible analysis of differences in cellular composition with crumblr.

Hoffman GE, Roussos P

Crumblr models cell-type count ratios using precision-weighted linear mixed models and multivariate tests across cell lineages. Simulations showed improved power with false-positive control; applications included aging single-cell datasets, supporting analysis of age-associated cellular composition rather than an intervention claim.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42778538

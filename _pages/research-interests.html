This week in context

  • AD targets and proteostasis: Human genetic prioritization and experimental validation nominate therapeutic targets, while worm studies probe intertissue control of protein aggregation. Neither establishes clinical treatment efficacy. [1] [2]
  • Immune aging: Sex-dependent thymic output in mice and population-scale human immune profiles highlight complementary mechanisms and associations. [3] [4]

Alzheimer’s Disease  ·  Aging

Alzheimer’s Disease

01 · Nature Neuroscience · 2026-10-08
Human genetics + experimental validation + health records

Alzheimer's disease target and drug discovery by leveraging multiomics and electronic health data.

Hou Y, Li Y, Zhang P et al.

Multiomics and Mendelian randomization nominated druggable AD targets, including EPHX2. Patient-derived neuronal models supported a protective variant, and EPHX2 inhibition improved cognition in 5xFAD mice. Health-record analyses identified drug associations with lower AD incidence, which do not establish treatment efficacy.

02 · Science Advances · 2026-10-09
Worm proteotoxicity and neuronal signaling

A neuronal GPCR modifies neuropeptide signaling to suppress proteostasis in distal tissues.

Hirbawi J, Zaretsky A, Zhu H et al.

Knockdown of the neuronal receptor gtr-1 altered neuropeptide-related expression, enhanced protein degradation, and affected aggregation differently across proteotoxic proteins. Building on an amyloid-β toxicity model, the experiments implicate intertissue signaling in proteostasis without demonstrating benefit in human AD.

Aging

03 · Nature Aging · 2026-10-09
Mouse immune aging with supporting human data

Lymph node contraction links sex-biased naive CD8+ T cell decline to compromised antigen recognition during middle age.

Menzel L, Zschummel M, O'Melia MJ et al.

Male-biased loss of naive CD8+ T cells and thymic involution contributed to lymph-node contraction and reduced antigen recognition. Androgen ablation restored thymic output and improved cancer-specific responses in male mice, identifying an experimental mechanism rather than a clinical recommendation.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42855600

04 · Immunity · 2026-10-09
Human single-cell cohorts + proteomic associations

Single-cell analyses of global cohorts outline determinants of immune aging and link the ratio of GZMK+ to GZMB+ Tem cells to health trajectories.

Terekhova M, Kleverov M, Francis T et al.

Integrated blood single-cell data from 2,609 individuals revealed age-, sex-, and ancestry-related immune variation. The GZMK+/GZMB+ effector-memory ratio reflected heterogeneous remodeling, while a GZMB+ signature was associated with inflammation, mortality, and disease risk across proteomic cohorts.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42854692

05 · Science Advances · 2026-10-07
Human engineered-vessel model of progeria

Human trilayer engineered blood vessel reveals influence of fibroblasts on disease progression in model of Hutchinson-Gilford progeria.

Shores KL, Gao XD, Liu DR et al.

Trilayer vessels containing endothelial cells, smooth muscle cells, and fibroblasts modeled vascular pathology in Hutchinson–Gilford progeria. Fibroblasts contributed to matrix dysregulation and fibrotic signaling; base editing reversed several abnormalities in vitro. The model addresses accelerated aging, not a proven intervention for normal aging.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42842666

06 · Science Advances · 2026-10-07
Exploratory giant-tortoise genomics and methylomics

Epigenetic insights into extreme longevity in the world's oldest terrestrial animal, Jonathan.

Vaisvil B, Schmitt DP, Jones A et al.

Analysis of the 194-year-old tortoise Jonathan, compared with other tortoises, identified distinctive genomic and methylation patterns. Low-entropy promoter regions were enriched for mitochondrial and RNA-processing pathways. This small comparative study generates longevity hypotheses rather than demonstrating causal mechanisms.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42842650

07 · Nature Aging · 2026-10-06
Cross-species lipidomics + worm lifespan experiments

Longer lipids mark aging and constrain lifespan.

Li W, Schomakers BV, Trętowicz MM et al.

Lipid acyl-chain length increased with age across multiple species and tissues. Analyses implicated the remodeler Plb1, and its knockdown reversed lipid elongation and extended worm lifespan. Human frailty genetics provided additional support, but human intervention benefits remain untested.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42839094

08 · Cell Reports · 2026-10-05
Human lifespan immune-cell reference atlas

Single-cell transcriptomic reference map of human peripheral blood γδ T cells from 1 year to 90 years reveals major changes before adulthood.

Yang T, León-Lara X, Almeida V et al.

A reference of 106,711 peripheral γδ T cells from 223 people aged 1–90 years revealed the largest compositional and transcriptional changes before adulthood. Major states were comparatively stable thereafter, emphasizing the importance of developmental age when interpreting immune-aging datasets.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42837259

09 · Nature Aging · 2026-10-05
Cellular senescence + aged-mouse experiments

Decline of chaperone-mediated autophagy in aging impairs macrophage clearance of senescent cells.

Sereda R, Lindenau K, Diaz A et al.

Declining chaperone-mediated autophagy altered senescent-cell secretions and impaired macrophage clearance. Macrophage-specific disruption increased senescent-cell accumulation, whereas pharmacological activation reduced burden in aged mice and disease severity in a pulmonary-fibrosis model. Clinical efficacy was not established.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42834177

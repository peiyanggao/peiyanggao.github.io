Alzheimer’s Disease

01 · Nature Communications · 2026-10-22
Multicenter clinical cohort + tissue mass spectrometry

Tau isoform mapping across tissues and biofluids reveals a signature distinguishing Alzheimer’s disease from amyotrophic lateral sclerosis

Abu-Rumeileh S, Großkopf H, Guthknecht A et al.

In a multicenter cohort, direct serum assays found brain-derived tau and p-tau217 elevated in both Alzheimer’s disease and amyotrophic lateral sclerosis. After brain-derived tau immunoprecipitation, brain-derived tau and brain-derived p-tau217 were elevated in Alzheimer’s disease only, improving discrimination from ALS. Tissue mass spectrometry also found peripheral big tau predominated in muscle, underscoring a potential source of blood-test confounding.

02 · Cell Reports · 2026-10-22
Microglial conditional knockout + PS19 tauopathy mice

TIA1 is required for microglial activation in tauopathy

Webber CJ, van der Spek SJF, Cruz AL et al.

In PS19 tauopathy mice, microglial TIA1 deletion reduced inflammatory cytokine and disease-associated microglial markers, alongside lower tau pathology. Proteomic analyses identified interactions between TIA1 and C1Q-related microglial proteins. These results are preclinical tauopathy findings and do not establish effects in people with Alzheimer’s disease.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42911815

03 · Science Advances · 2026-10-21
BCG vaccination in a PS19 tauopathy mouse model

BCG vaccination mitigates tau pathology and restores cognitive function in PS19 mice

Shee S, Huang M, Baghel MS et al.

In PS19 mice, BCG treatment reduced hippocampal phosphorylated tau and microgliosis, preserved neuronal markers, and improved performance on a novel-object-recognition task. The study also reported changes in hippocampal gene expression and myeloid-cell activity. These findings support a preclinical tauopathy hypothesis, not evidence that BCG prevents Alzheimer’s disease in people.

Aging

04 · Nature Aging · 2026-10-23
Single-cell atlas across 12 human tissues + model validation

Uncovering the transcriptional hallmarks of endothelial cell aging via integrated single-cell analysis

Dobner S, Kleissl L, Tóth F et al.

An integrated atlas of more than 375,000 endothelial cells from 12 human tissues identified age-associated transcriptional shifts, including lower angiogenic signatures in venous endothelial cells and changes in extracellular-matrix and mechanotransduction programs. The authors also linked a p21-associated program with cellular senescence in cell and animal models.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42919924

05 · Cell Metabolism · 2026-10-23
Osteocyte Piezo1 deletion + aged-mouse loading experiments

Dynamic force sensing by bone delays aging and extends lifespan

Zhang Z, Cai Z, Liu S et al.

Deleting the mechanosensory channel Piezo1 in osteocytes accelerated whole-body aging in mice. Dynamic tibial loading in mice aged 20–24 months extended healthspan and lifespan (9.4%, or 79.5 days) and reversed bone-marrow immunosenescence. The study also identified PEDF as a mechano-responsive factor associated with healthy aging in human data.

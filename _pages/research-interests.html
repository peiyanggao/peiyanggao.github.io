Alzheimer’s Disease

01 · Nature Aging · 2026-09-14
Human brain tissue + functional experiments

A microprotein atlas of the human frontal cortex in Alzheimer's disease

Miller B, Vieira de Souza E, Lau C et al.

An atlas integrating more than 600 postmortem frontal cortex samples identified 1,067 microproteins absent from reviewed UniProtKB entries. A 63-amino-acid microprotein encoded at MKKS was reduced in Alzheimer’s disease; its loss impaired microglial mitochondrial respiration.

02 · Nature Genetics · 2026-09-14
Human genetics · Research letter

APOE-stratified genome-wide association analyses provide insights into the genetic etiology of Alzheimers's disease

Thomassen JQ, Leonard H, Ulms B et al.

GWAS analyses stratified by APOE ε4 and ε2 carrier status revealed nine new AD risk signals. DDHD1 stood out for a protective association in ε4 carriers and supporting expression-related evidence.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42736378

03 · Nature Aging · 2026-09-11
Human tissue + vascular models + in vivo experiments

Fibronectin mediates APOE4-driven blood-brain barrier dysfunction in Alzheimer's disease

Bhattarai P, Yilmaz E, Cakir EÖ et al.

Human tissue and experimental models implicated astrocyte-derived fibronectin in APOE4-associated blood–brain barrier dysfunction. Excess fibronectin disrupted growth-factor signaling; reducing fibronectin or restoring signaling rescued barrier function in the tested models.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42728404

04 · Nature Medicine · 2026-09-11
Longitudinal human cohorts

Joint impact of pathological burden and cognitive resilience on Alzheimer's disease risk

Wang Z, Wang S, Liang Y et al.

Among 3,119 older adults followed for a median of 13.7 years, longitudinal pathology and cognitive-resilience indices independently predicted incident AD dementia. Higher pathology was associated with greater risk (HR 2.50 per SD), while greater resilience was associated with lower risk (HR 0.51 per SD); findings were replicated in another cohort.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42728380

05 · Nature Neuroscience · 2026-09-11
Human imaging, sleep physiology, and replication

Human tau pathology is associated with lonely, nontraveling slow waves linked to memory impairment

Sharon O, Chen X, Dude J et al.

Frontal tau pathology was associated with less coordinated, less widely traveling non-REM slow waves and poorer memory retention. The investigators used tau PET and replicated and extended the findings with CSF pathology markers in an independent clinical cohort.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42728376

Aging

06 · Cell Metabolism · 2026-09-10
Human cells + aged mice

Blocking PD-L2 prevents senescent cell accumulation and age-related dysfunction

Chaib S, Langhi Prata LGP, Suda M et al.

PD-L2 expression increased in senescent human cells and during aging. Older PD-L2-knockout mice accumulated fewer senescent cells and showed greater insulin sensitivity and grip strength; anti-PD-L2 treatment restored insulin sensitivity in aged wild-type mice.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42721966

07 · Science Immunology · 2026-09-11
Aged-mouse vaccine experiments

AMnESTI reverses age-associated lung immune decline to drive heterosubtypic protection by inactivated influenza vaccines

Gao S, Zhang Q, Banstola A et al.

An intranasal formulation targeting alveolar epithelial cells with a STING agonist remodeled aged lung immunity. With an inactivated influenza vaccine, a single dose conferred heterosubtypic protection against the representative strains tested in aged mice.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42726884

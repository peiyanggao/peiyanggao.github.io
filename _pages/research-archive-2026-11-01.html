Alzheimer’s Disease

01 · Neuron · 2026-10-27
Human Alzheimer’s disease data + 3×Tg-AD mouse circuit experiments

Trait-linked brainstem circuit resilience balances tau pathology-induced cellular vulnerability in Alzheimer's disease

Zheng JQ, Fan HW, Yao C et al.

In 3×Tg-AD mice, tau accumulation in the Edinger–Westphal nucleus was linked to Kcnma1 upregulation, hypoexcitability in CART+ neurons, and impaired attention; this vulnerability predicted future decline in mice and people with Alzheimer’s disease. In mice, exploratory behavior was associated with activation of a compensatory medial parabrachial–Edinger–Westphal circuit through substance P–Tacr1 signaling, which sustained neuronal function and delayed cognitive deterioration. The circuit findings are preclinical.

Aging

02 · Science Advances · 2026-10-28
Aging mouse brain + mechanistic microglial experiments

An oxidized ribonucleotide 8-oxoGTP drives neuroinflammation and accelerates cognitive decline

Li J, Wang ZH, Pan JX et al.

8-oxoGTP accumulated in the aging mouse brain and activated microglial G-protein signaling. Experimental elevation induced neuroinflammation, synaptic dysfunction, tau hyperphosphorylation, neuronal loss, and cognitive decline; Src-family kinase inhibition mitigated neuroinflammatory and cognitive effects in the model. These findings are preclinical.

03 · Immunity · 2026-10-29
Age-progressive mouse phenotype + human pathway observations

Growth factor VEGF-B sustains intestinal Tfh cell stemness to restrict Taurinivorans outgrowth and microbiota-driven obesity

Zhou JA, Tu J, Yue J et al.

In T cell-specific Flt1-deletion mice, spontaneous obesity emerged with advancing age. Flt1 deletion impaired T follicular helper cell fitness and gut IgA, allowing Taurinivorans to expand and deplete taurine. The study links VEGFR-1–AKT signaling to T-cell stemness by limiting p53-mediated senescence and reports related pathway evidence in diet-induced obese mice and humans.

04 · Cell Reports · 2026-10-29
Cross-species recordings pooled from 20 laboratories

Locus coeruleus noradrenergic neurons have diverse activity patterns across species and biological context

Kelberman MA, Rodberg E, Munn BR et al.

A pooled analysis of locus-coeruleus recordings across vertebrates found firing rates varied by species and were modulated by age, sex, and recording context. Activity patterns formed a continuum rather than two discrete modes, with subregions associated with biological factors and neurodegenerative models. The study identifies age as one contributor to variation in noradrenergic activity.

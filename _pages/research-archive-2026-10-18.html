Alzheimer’s Disease

01 · Science Advances · 2026-10-16
Mouse neuromodulation + traumatic brain injury and AD models

Noninvasive ultrashort wave neuromodulation enhances glymphatic clearance to alleviate traumatic brain edema and Alzheimer's pathology.

Liu J, Xu C, Ruan H et al.

In mice, 40.68-MHz ultrashort-wave stimulation increased glymphatic flow alongside delta-wave synchronization and aquaporin-4 polarization. It also reduced traumatic brain injury-related edema and improved cognitive deficits in Alzheimer’s-model mice, supporting a preclinical mechanism rather than a demonstrated human treatment.

02 · Neuron · 2026-10-15
Mouse interneuron imaging + electrophysiology

Parvalbumin cell state dynamics regulate gamma oscillations and are disrupted in Alzheimer's disease.

Ma K, Park K, Honma PS et al.

Awake-mouse recordings showed spontaneous gamma oscillations coincide with transitions among parvalbumin interneuron states. These transitions were disrupted in J20 Alzheimer’s-model mice; increasing Nav1.1 expression restored interneuron dynamics, gamma activity, and network stability.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42880289

03 · Cell Stem Cell · 2026-10-15
CRISPR screen + APOE4 cell, organoid, and mouse models

Genome-wide CRISPR screen reveals that Wnt signaling regulates lipid accumulation in APOE4 oligodendrocytes.

Akay LA, Bright A, Dong D et al.

Genome-wide CRISPR screening in iPSC-derived APOE4 oligodendrocytes identified reduced Wnt signaling and excess GSK3B activity in lipid-droplet accumulation. GSK3B inhibition reduced lipid droplets and improved myelination in organoids and APOE4;PS19 mice; efficacy in people remains unknown.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42880287

04 · Science Advances · 2026-10-14
Human endothelial cells + Alzheimer’s mouse models

Mitochondrial carbonic anhydrase-VB inhibition rescues brain endothelial stress and memory in Alzheimer's disease models.

Lemon NL, Canepa E, Vázquez-Torres R et al.

Pharmacologic CA-V inhibition and CA-VB knockout protected human cerebral microvascular endothelial cells against amyloid-β-related mitochondrial injury, barrier loss, and inflammation. In 3xTg Alzheimer’s-model mice, inhibition reduced endothelial and caspase activation and preserved memory.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42873864

05 · Science Translational Medicine · 2026-10-14
Preclinical studies + randomized phase 1 trial

Preclinical and first-in-human evaluation of VG-3927/SAR448851, an oral small-molecule TREM2 agonist for Alzheimer's disease.

Dejanovic B, Figley MD, Weisman E et al.

Preclinical models and a randomized, placebo-controlled phase 1 study (n=115) evaluated oral TREM2 agonist VG-3927. The trial supported tolerability up to 140 mg and CNS penetration; dose-related CSF sTREM2 reductions indicated target engagement, not clinical efficacy.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42873892

06 · Nature Aging · 2026-10-12
Human postmortem cohort + methylation profiling

Brain DNA methylation, APOE genotype and Alzheimer's disease neuropathology in a large postmortem cohort.

Miao G, Gao C, Yu L et al.

Among 1,250 postmortem prefrontal cortex samples, the study identified 107 CpG probes associated with Alzheimer’s neuropathology, 38 of which replicated in an independent cohort. Methylation analyses estimated partial mediation of the APOE genotype–diagnosis association, and some implicated genes showed cell-type-specific expression.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42864517

07 · Nature Neuroscience · 2026-10-12
Human structural-variant genetics + functional analyses

Genome-wide analysis of common structural variants in Alzheimer's disease identifies a risk-associated regulatory CCG repeat in FAM193B.

Cao H, Fu Y, Zhou X et al.

Long-read and short-read Chinese cohort data identified a FAM193B promoter CCG-repeat expansion associated with Alzheimer’s disease (odds ratio 2.20 per 10-repeat increase). Expression analyses linked the repeat to FAM193B expression, while neuronal data associated elevated expression with DNA-damage and cell-death pathways.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42864446

08 · Neuron · 2026-10-12
Human postmortem tissue + sex-specific mouse models

The histone variant H2A.Z regulates sex differences in Alzheimer's disease pathophysiology.

Hategan LA, Luo JQ, Creighton SD et al.

Human postmortem hippocampus and 5xFAD mice showed sex-dependent shifts in H2A.Z occupancy. In mice, H2A.Z.1 depletion had different, stage-dependent effects on memory and pathology across sexes, highlighting context-specific biology.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42863472

Aging

09 · Nature Neuroscience · 2026-10-15
Human cognitive-resilience data + mouse aging experiments

An aging-sensitive secreted phospholipase confers neuroprotection and cognitive resilience.

Vicidomini C, Goode TD, Yu R et al.

Mouse experiments identified age-related upregulation of neuronal Pla2g2f; its loss worsened synaptic, glial, lipid, neurogenesis, and memory changes, whereas boosting it protected hippocampal-cortical circuits. Human data associated the phospholipase with cognitive resilience.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42881225

10 · Nature Metabolism · 2026-10-14
Cell redox models + aged mice

Genetic modulation of NAD(P)H homeostasis reveals distinct redox-coupled metabolic vulnerabilities.

Pan X, Zuckerman AL, Miller KN et al.

In primary fibroblasts, NADH reductive stress coincided with NADPH depletion and senescence features. Expanding the NADP(H) pool with SaNADK restored several metabolic and proliferation measures in cells; hepatic SaNADK partly improved inflammatory and metabolic expression signatures in aged male mice.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42875582

11 · Cell Metabolism · 2026-10-14
Aging, disuse, and cachexia mouse models

An NAD+-responsive adipose signal coordinates inter-organ defense against muscle atrophy.

Liang N, He J, Guo X et al.

Mouse experiments identified adipose-derived Serpina3n as a mediator of NAD+-linked muscle protection. The adipose-to-muscle signal supported muscle metabolism across aging, disuse, and cancer-cachexia models; clinical benefit in people has not been tested.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42874585

12 · Science Translational Medicine · 2026-10-14
Human lifespan metabolomics + longitudinal follow-up

Metabolomic aging and mortality clocks based on ensemble learning identify systemic aging risk across the human life span in 18,476 individuals.

Cao L, Chen Y, Zhang W et al.

Metabolomic profiles from 18,476 people aged 1–96 years supported models of metabolomic age and age deviation. Greater metabolomic age acceleration tracked less favorable health and lifestyle profiles and was associated with incident chronic disease and mortality over follow-up.

Journal article ↗ · PubMed PMID: 42873893

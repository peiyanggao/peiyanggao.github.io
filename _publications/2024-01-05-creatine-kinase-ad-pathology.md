This cross-sectional study used Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative data from 1,607 participants to examine whether creatine kinase (CK), a marker related to energy metabolism, was associated with cognitive status and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) biomarkers of Alzheimer's disease pathology.

**Key findings:**

- CK levels differed by age, sex, Alzheimer's disease diagnosis, cognitive status, and CSF biomarker group.
- Participants with Alzheimer's disease had lower CK levels than those with mild cognitive impairment.
- Amyloid-positive and phosphorylated-tau-positive groups had lower CK levels than their biomarker-negative counterparts.
- In adjusted linear regression, lower CK was associated with higher CSF phosphorylated tau, whereas the association with CSF amyloid-β was not statistically significant.

The findings identify an association between peripheral CK and tau-related Alzheimer's disease pathology, while the cross-sectional design does not establish causality.

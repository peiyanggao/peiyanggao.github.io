This study examined how antemortem amyloid, tau, and neurodegeneration biomarkers measured by positron emission tomography (PET), cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), and plasma correspond to Alzheimer's disease neuropathology at autopsy in 100 participants from the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative.

**Key findings:**

- Combined PET- and CSF-based A/T/N profiles showed high concordance with the stage of Alzheimer's disease neuropathological change.
- PET- and CSF-based biomarkers individually discriminated autopsy-confirmed Alzheimer's disease with high accuracy.
- Plasma A/T/N biomarkers showed better discriminative performance when combined with APOE ε4 genotype than when used alone.
- Amyloid PET and CSF p-tau181/Aβ42 were most consistent with amyloid pathology, whereas tau PET and CSF p-tau181/Aβ42 were most consistent with tau pathology.

These findings support PET- and CSF-based A/T/N profiles as useful tools for identifying the presence and stage of Alzheimer's disease neuropathology, while indicating that plasma biomarkers may benefit from integration with genetic risk information.

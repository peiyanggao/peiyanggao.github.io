This prospective cohort study used wrist accelerometry in 73,411 UK Biobank participants to quantify physical activity and sedentary behavior and examine their associations with incident neuropsychiatric diseases and related biological phenotypes.

**Key findings:**

- Moderate-to-vigorous physical activity energy expenditure showed the strongest inverse associations with neuropsychiatric disease risk.
- A greater proportion of sedentary time was associated with higher risks of several neuropsychiatric outcomes.
- Dose-response analyses indicated L-shaped associations for physical activity and an inverted L-shaped association between sedentary time and dementia.
- Physical activity and sedentary behavior were associated with cognitive, physical, and mental-health measures and with structural differences in regions including the lateral occipital cortex, cuneus, pallidum, and nucleus accumbens.
- Proteomic and metabolic analyses highlighted ITGAV and a lipoprotein-related signal, while structural equation models implicated inflammatory and metabolic pathways.

The study provides multidimensional observational evidence linking device-measured activity patterns to brain health and incident neuropsychiatric disease.

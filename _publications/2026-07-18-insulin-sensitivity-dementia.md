This prospective cohort study included 290,898 dementia-free UK Biobank participants and assessed the estimated glucose disposal rate (eGDR), a clinically accessible indicator of insulin sensitivity, in relation to incident all-cause dementia, Alzheimer's disease, and vascular dementia.

**Key findings:**

- During a mean follow-up of 13.23 years, 4,794 participants developed all-cause dementia, 2,138 developed Alzheimer's disease, and 1,070 developed vascular dementia.
- Higher eGDR was non-linearly associated with lower risks of all three dementia outcomes.
- Each one-unit increase in eGDR was associated with lower risks of all-cause dementia, Alzheimer's disease, and vascular dementia.
- Participants in the highest eGDR quartile had lower dementia risks than those in the lowest quartile.
- eGDR modified the association between Alzheimer's disease polygenic risk and incident Alzheimer's disease; participants with higher eGDR and lower genetic risk had the lowest observed risk.

These findings identify insulin sensitivity as a potentially modifiable correlate of dementia risk, while causal effects require confirmation in intervention studies.
